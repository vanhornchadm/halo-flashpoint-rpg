*Between firefights, survival depends on what you notice, what you know, and what you’re willing to try.*

## Describe Your Approach

Tell the GM what your character wants to accomplish and how they attempt it. Equipment, training, species, relationships, and circumstances determine what is possible.

Straightforward actions succeed without a roll. Impossible actions require a different approach. **Roll only when the outcome is uncertain and failure would matter.**

## Attributes

| Attribute | Used for |
|---|---|
| **Strength (STR)** | Lifting, carrying, forcing obstacles, exerting physical power, and determining hand-thrown [[keywords/grenade|Grenade]] range. |
| **Agility (AGL)** | Stealth, balance, coordination, and precise movement. |
| **Intelligence (INT)** | Technical tasks, knowledge, reasoning, and analysis. |
| **Awareness (AW)** | Noticing danger, searching, tracking, and reading immediate cues. |
| **Will (WIL)** | Resolve, concentration, and resisting fear, coercion, or mental influence. |
| **Fellowship (FEL)** | Persuasion, deception, negotiation, and building trust. |
| **Luck (LCK)** | Favorable circumstances beyond the character’s control. |

An attribute of **0** is average. Positive values add dice; negative values remove dice.

Use the attribute that best fits the character’s approach. A threatening display of physical force might use Strength, while a carefully worded warning might use Fellowship. Changing the attribute requires changing the approach.

## Making a Test

1. **Establish the stakes.** The GM explains what success accomplishes and the likely consequence of failure.
2. **Choose an attribute.** Roll **3d8**, adding or removing dice equal to that attribute. Always roll at least one die.
3. **Count successes.** Each result of **5 or higher** is one success. Each **8** also generates another die; further 8s continue generating dice.
4. **Compare successes to the difficulty.**

| Difficulty | Successes required |
|---|---:|
| Favorable, but still uncertain | 1 |
| Standard challenge | 2 |
| Difficult challenge | 3 |

If the task is straightforward, skip the roll instead of assigning a difficulty.

**Example:** A Brute with STR +2 tries to force a damaged hatch. The GM sets a standard challenge, requiring 2 successes. The player rolls 5d8. Results of 2, 4, 5, 6, and 7 produce 3 successes—the hatch opens.

## Circumstances and Assistance

Useful equipment, preparation, or a favorable position may make an action possible, remove the need for a roll, or reduce its difficulty. Adverse circumstances may increase the difficulty. The GM considers the situation as a whole rather than adding numerous modifiers.

An ally who describes useful assistance may grant **+1 die**. Only one assistance die applies to a test, regardless of how many characters help. Helping may expose the ally to the same consequences.

The GM normally calls for one roll to resolve a shared attempt. Additional characters do not each roll simply to repeat the same effort.

## Success, Failure, and Trying Again

Meeting the difficulty achieves the stated goal.

Failure produces the established consequence: lost time, noise, damaged equipment, suspicion, a worse position, or failure to obtain what was sought. It need not stop the adventure.

Do not repeat the same test until someone succeeds. Another attempt requires a meaningful change in approach, tools, information, or circumstances—or acceptance of a new cost or risk.

## Using Luck

*Sometimes the right weapon really is lying beside the right door.*

Luck determines unresolved circumstances that no character’s skill controls. The GM may call for a Luck test, and players may suggest a suitable opportunity.

Examples include:

- Whether an abandoned vehicle has usable fuel.
- Whether recovered supplies contain needed ammunition.
- Whether a dropped object lands somewhere accessible.

Roll Luck like any other attribute: **3d8 plus LCK dice**, with successes on 5+ and additional dice on 8s.

Luck cannot replace another attribute test, reroll a failed action, contradict an established fact, or make an impossible event occur.

Resolve each circumstance once. For a shared stroke of fortune, the GM identifies the character most directly affected; the whole party does not roll separately. Assistance does not apply to Luck.

**Example:** Awareness determines whether you find a concealed supply case. If its contents have not already been established, Luck might determine whether it contains something particularly useful.

## Social Interaction

Describe what you say and what you offer, threaten, conceal, or appeal to. Roll Fellowship only when the response is uncertain and matters.

A successful test achieves a plausible social outcome. It does not compel someone to abandon fundamental loyalties, believe an obvious impossibility, or obey without limits.

Awareness can reveal observable signs of discomfort or deception; it does not automatically reveal thoughts or establish the truth.

Players retain control of their characters’ choices.

## Species, Bodies, and Artificial Intelligences

Attributes do not grant abilities a character’s body or equipment cannot support. High Intelligence does not provide access to a disconnected system, and high Strength does not let a [[keywords/digital|Digital]] AI lift an object.

On a profile:

- **—** means related checks automatically pass or fail where the relevant species or ability rule specifies the outcome. The dash alone does not choose the result or grant immunity. For Flood WIL checks, [[keywords/collective-will|Collective Will]] specifies automatic success.
- **\*** means the value comes from another profile or a specific rule. Identify that value before rolling. If its source is undefined, the GM establishes it before the attribute is used.

AIs require suitable access to interact with electronic systems. Attachment alone does not grant control of a host’s body; hosting and possession follow their own rules.

## Combat and Special Orders

Use **RA, FI, and SV** and the normal combat rules for attacks and defenses. Roleplay attributes do not automatically add dice to those tests.

An environmental or technical task during combat may use these roleplay rules, but still costs whatever action the GM assigns. This section grants no free combat actions.

Outside combat, Special Orders provide inspiration for what characters might attempt. They cost no Command Dice, but their automatic combat benefits do not apply: resolve the activity through ordinary roleplay and an attribute test when needed.

Characters do not need a matching Special Order to attempt an otherwise plausible action.

**No roleplay points, spending pools, or refresh rules are required.**
